Preventive skin care, rethought for the era of health intelligence

The core of preventive care is not starting treatments early. It is understanding how your skin is changing and stepping in only at the right time and only as much as needed, so that unnecessary treatment is reduced. This column was written by Dr. Lee Doochan of Reyeonk Clinic in Apgujeong.

At a glance
  • There is no single age that is the right answer for everyone to start anti-aging care.
  • We look at skin condition before age: moisture and barrier, pigment and blood vessels, dermal density, fat distribution and facial movement.
  • Prevention does not mean repeating strong treatments from a young age. It means telling apart what to do now from what can wait.
  • Recording your starting condition and the reason for treatment, then re-evaluating by the same criteria, helps reduce unnecessary treatment.
  • Whether treatment is needed and how it goes vary between individuals, so it is decided at a consultation after seeing your skin in person.

People who manage health with information, not hunches

These days people track how long they slept, how much they moved and what they ate. Judging your health by numbers and records rather than vague feelings has become an everyday habit. The same shift is happening in aesthetic medicine. Patients no longer stop at “please make me look better.” They want to know why their skin is dry, which layer has lost firmness, and which care is needed now and which can wait.

This attitude can be called health intelligence. Knowing a lot about health is not enough. What matters is the ability to read your own condition, tell what information really means, and apply it to daily life and treatment choices.

Longer lives have brought prevention forward

One of the most regrettable situations in practice is meeting women in their late forties after sagging has already advanced considerably. The late twenties is a lovely age, and many things can be corrected relatively easily then. But they spent their younger years without knowing that was possible, and later come in saying, “I thought this couldn't be done.” Each time I hear that, I wish they had understood their own condition a little earlier.

That said, “prevention” does not mean repeating strong treatments from a young age. For someone whose skin barrier is unsettled, recovery may come first, and a person whose problem is expression habits needs a different approach from someone whose fat distribution or skin firmness has changed. Prevention that does not distinguish the current condition can simply add cost and burden.

Care that fits your condition, not your age

Even in the same decade, concerns differ. One person's issue is recurring inflammation and pigmentation, while another first notices fine lines around the eyes or a change in the jawline. So rather than choosing treatments by age alone, we look together at skin moisture and barrier, pigment and blood vessels, dermal density, fat distribution and facial movement.

What we check first at Reyeonk is not the name of a device. We start by asking what change bothers you most, what impression you want to keep, and how much recovery time your daily life can allow. Then we choose the one highest-priority step for your current condition, and connect to the next step only when necessary.

Good preventive care leaves records

In preventive care, what matters is the direction of change rather than the result of a single treatment. Record the starting condition and the reason for treatment, and after some time evaluate again by the same criteria. Only then can you judge whether the effect is sufficient, whether maintenance is needed, and whether the next treatment can wait.

Photos and skin records in this process are not material for recommending more treatments. They should be evidence for checking whether an earlier choice was appropriate and for removing treatments that are not needed. Patients, too, need to understand how they have changed so that the next decision is made on judgment rather than anxiety.

Not missing the right time, and reducing unnecessary treatment

There is no age that is the right answer for everyone to start anti-aging care. We need to check, in order, whether there is a change that bothers you now, whether that change can be managed with lifestyle care alone, and if medical intervention is needed, how much is appropriate.

Preventive care, as I see it, does not mean treating without question from a young age. It means knowing in advance what you can do now and what you do not yet need to do, so that you do not pass the best time unknowingly and regret it later. Not missing the time that matters and reducing unnecessary treatment: that is the purpose of preventive care.

Questions to consider before a consultation

  • Which part have you noticed changing first?
  • Rather than a younger-looking face, what impression do you want to keep for longer?
  • Which suits you better: one big change, or gradual changes with less burden?

Whether treatment is needed and how it goes vary between individuals. What to do now and what to postpone is decided at a consultation after seeing your skin in person.

8 common questions

  • Thermage FLX or Ulthera: which is right for me?

    If the aim is to lift sagging, Ulthera; if the concern is skin firmness, texture and fine lines, Thermage FLX. Ulthera uses ultrasound (HIFU) to work on the SMAS fascia layer, and Thermage FLX uses radiofrequency (RF) to stimulate dermal collagen. The two can also be done on the same day as “Ulthermage,” which treats both layers. Which combination suits you is decided at a consultation with the doctor.

  • How long do the effects of Thermage FLX and Ulthera last?

    Thermage FLX usually lasts 10 to 12 months and Ulthera 8 to 9 months on average. The effect becomes most noticeable one to two months after treatment, while new collagen builds up, and varies between individuals with skin condition, age and lifestyle.

  • Can I fly right after treatment in Seoul?

    For Botox, fillers, skin boosters, Sunsak injection, Thermage FLX and Ulthera, travel on the same day is generally not a problem. After VirtueRF, Potenza and Eyethera, redness may remain for 1 to 3 days. We suggest allowing about a week of recovery after thread lifting, and within a week after liposuction. Please be sure to tell us your departure schedule at the consultation.

  • How can I confirm that Ulthera or Thermage is genuine?

    Genuine Ulthera uses equipment certified by Merz Aesthetics, and the NFC tag on the tip lets you verify authenticity. Ask to see the device and tip yourself before treatment. Reyeonk Clinic is an official Merz-certified partner and uses only genuine devices and tips.

  • Can I have treatment on the same day as my consultation?

    Yes, in many cases treatment is possible on the day of your consultation. Most non-surgical treatments, including Thermage FLX, Ulthera, Sunsak injection, Botox and fillers, can be done the same day. If your schedule is short, please let us know when you book.

  • Are Ulthera and Thermage very painful?

    Honestly, there is some pain. However, it can be made much more comfortable by lengthening the numbing cream time or choosing gas or sleep anesthesia, and if you are sensitive to pain we will adjust the energy together at the consultation.
