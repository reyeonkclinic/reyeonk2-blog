Do you notice enlarged pores around your nose, or foundation settling into your pores over time?

If rough texture and sagging pores don't improve even with plenty of moisturiser and exfoliation, Potenza, which stimulates regeneration from within the skin, may be worth considering.

Why do pores look enlarged?

The reasons pores look wider differ from person to person. Heavy sebum production can widen the pore opening, and reduced skin elasticity can make pores look stretched vertically.

Acne scars and a damaged skin barrier can also leave the texture uneven. Rather than judging by pore size alone, it helps to look at sebum levels, elasticity, scar depth and barrier condition together.

Which treatments can be considered for enlarged pores?

The approach depends on the cause. Potenza delivers radiofrequency (RF) energy to the dermis through fine needles, aiming to trigger collagen production and skin regeneration.

It is designed to limit stimulation at the skin surface while delivering heat energy to the dermis. It may be considered when elasticity loss or scarring is also present. Even with the same device, results vary with tip selection, needle depth and RF intensity, so a customised plan from an experienced medical team matters.

What is the difference between 600 and 1,000 shots?

A shot count is the number of times the tip is placed and energy is delivered during treatment. With the same tip over the same area, more shots allow the treatment points to be spaced more densely.

If the concern is limited to a small area, such as beside the nose, the plan can focus there. If it runs from beside the nose to both cheeks, shots can be distributed across a wider area.

1,000 shots refers to a number that can deliver RF energy at sufficient density across the whole face. More shots are not always better, though. At the consultation, the doctor considers skin thickness, where pores stand out and whether scars are present, then decides the shot count, needle depth and RF intensity together.

What happens if I combine Potenza pumping tips with skin botox?

While Potenza targets the regeneration response in the dermis, skin botox is often described as a method focused on surface skin condition, such as texture and oil control.

When considering both, it is worth discussing with the doctor whether their goals overlap and whether each suits your current skin. Confirm in advance exactly what the combined package includes.

Frequently asked questions

  • Which skin concerns is Potenza suited to?

    Potenza is considered for enlarged pores, acne scars, depressed scars, reduced skin elasticity, fine lines and skin texture. Whether it suits your concern depends on an assessment of your skin, decided with the doctor.

  • What should I do before a Potenza treatment?

    Stop using retinol and acid-based products two weeks before treatment. Treatment may be restricted during pregnancy or breastfeeding, for keloid-prone skin, or if there is a metal implant near the treatment area. On the day, come without heavy makeup. If you have a history of herpes, tell the specialist in advance. If you have severe inflammatory acne or a badly damaged skin barrier, it is safer to calm the skin first.

  • How long does recovery take after Potenza?

    Redness, warmth and minor swelling or crusting may occur right after treatment. Crusts usually fall off naturally after 3–5 days, and most people can resume daily activities from the next day. Do not pick at crusts. For several days avoid scrubs and exfoliation, and apply moisturiser and sunscreen carefully.

  • How many sessions do I need?

    There is no fixed number. Sessions are usually spaced 4–6 weeks apart, and the direction often becomes clear around 3–4 sessions. Retouches can continue as needed. Results vary, so confirm a plan that suits your skin at your consultation.
